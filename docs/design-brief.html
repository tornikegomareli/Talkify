The product

One gesture, one surface, no window

Talkify has no main window and no Dock icon. The whole product is a key you hold and a black shape that drops out of the notch while you hold it. This is the only interaction that ships today — everything else in the roadmap is gated behind an insertion-latency benchmark.

0 ms

Press Fn

The session starts immediately. The app has already prewarmed the speech model, so there is nothing to wait for.

< 250 ms release

Tap to latch

A quick tap leaves dictation running hands-free until the next press. Holding keeps it open only while held.

while speaking

HUD reacts

The shape shows a live voice visual instead of the draft text. Volatile words render instantly; only finalized text is ever inserted.

on release

Insert

The text is pasted into the captured target, then the previous clipboard is restored if it has not changed.

escape / 15 s

Bail out

Escape cancels without inserting. No speech in the first 15 seconds and the session closes silently.

Refusals and errors use the same surface: a secure password field shows “Secure field”, missing permissions show their own line. Messages clear themselves after about two seconds. There is no notification, no alert, no second window.

Surface

The notch island

One surface, one behavior, every display. A MacBook with a real notch gets measured; every other display gets a simulated notch of the same stand-in footprint, minus the corner fillets that only make sense against real hardware. The window never resizes — only its origin moves — so it is sized once for the tallest layout it can ever reach.

The fillet is a square with a quarter disc cut from its outer corner, so the black stays full height where it meets the flank and tapers as it runs into the bezel. Cut the other corner and it reads as a detached tab.

Rules that cannot bend

Click-through and non-activating — the HUD never takes focus, and clicks land on the app behind it. It sits above full-screen apps and on every Space. It always descends from the top center; never the bottom. The display is locked at session start and follows only if that display disconnects. Bounce is expressed in top-anchored scale, never position: a position overshoot would open a gap against the screen edge.

Silence is not a dead microphone

If levels stop arriving for 600 ms while listening, a watchdog flips every visual to a motionless amber state. Silence sits near zero and keeps moving; a dead microphone stops and turns orange. Switch the bar at the top of this page to Dead mic and every demo below changes together.

Voice visuals · pick one of two

Waveform, or Edge Glow

While listening, the animated visual replaces the draft text entirely. Two families ship, chosen in Settings. The waveform has nine styles; the Edge Glow has six palettes crossed with three centers. Both are fed by the same level history: 56 samples, roughly 46 per second, smoothed with a fast attack and slow release.

Waveform — nine styles

Every style draws the same buffer; only the mark changes. Chart Line ships as the default and carries its own treatment — a crisp core over two glow passes, a mirrored floor reflection, and a specular band travelling along the line. The other eight run through a shared sheen shader.

Edge Glow — the triptych

Three animations, one visual. A palette-gradient beam blooms out from under the housing, sweeps the shape's open silhouette (left flank, bottom, right flank — never the hidden top edge), and follows the voice in brightness and stroke weight. A one-shot ripple rolls across the black housing at session start. A particle cloud in the same palette chases the beam's origin.

Center

Edge Glow Feel test

Beam: 6 pt stroke swelling to 12 with the voice, 12 pt blur, 28 pt spill beyond the shape, one full sweep every 4 seconds, 0.4 s bloom-in and drain-out. Particles: 32 motes, 3–12 pt, respawning when they reach the origin. Ripple: 3 pt displacement, ~0.45 s to cross the shape.

Reduce Motion Shipping

System Reduce Motion replaces both visuals with a 4 pt level bar and keeps the draft text visible. Expand and collapse become a 120 ms fade. This is the entire accessibility fallback today — no app-level toggle duplicates the system setting.

Dead microphone Shipping

Amber, motionless, in every visual: the waveform flattens to an orange line, the beam drops its palette, the particle cloud zeroes out instantly. The colour is #FF9929 and it is the only non-neutral hue the HUD uses outside the glow palettes.

Motion

How it arrives, and how it grows

Two motion preferences ship. Reveal style decides how the shape enters and leaves. Long-draft behaviour decides what happens when the words outrun one line. Both were picked by feel and all variants stayed selectable.

Reveal styles

Long draft behaviour

Grow Down won the feel test and ships as the default. The other two stayed as preferences rather than being deleted. Head truncation is deliberate on the single-line variants: the newest words are the ones a speaker checks.

Decided, not yet built

Two decisions are written down and still waiting on code. Settings should become a borderless, fixed-size, centred modal with its own header, a top-right close control, Escape to dismiss, and header dragging — today it is still a standard titled window. And a dictation session should snapshot its appearance and sound choices at start, so changing a picker mid-session no longer alters a running visual; today the HUD reads the live store.

The window is dark-only by decision. The accent is fixed Talkify blue #73A6FF — a selected glow palette never recolours the Settings shell.

GPU layer

Four Metal programs

Three are SwiftUI shader hooks applied over pixels the view hierarchy already drew. The fourth is a real compute pipeline with its own MTKView inside the click-through panel, because a particle system needs state that survives between frames and a pixel shader cannot carry any.

EdgeGlow.metal

colorEffect
Masks the drawn beam by distance from a moving origin: exp(−d²) × amplitude, spreading outward as the session ramp grows. Amplitude is the voice — resting at 1.8, flaring to 3.0 while speaking. This shader draws nothing itself; it decides how much of the SwiftUI beam shows.

WaveformSheen.metal

layerEffect
A finish over eight of the nine waveform styles: chromatic fringing that grows from the centre outward and with the voice, a four-tap bloom so bright bars halo, and a metallic specular band ping-ponging across the strip every 2.8 s. Chart Line and Siri Wave opt out — they carry their own treatment.

Ripple.metal

layerEffect
The WWDC24 ripple, unmodified: one wave expanding from the glow origin, displacing pixels radially and brightening them. It runs once per session start, across the black housing only, and sits inside the clip so the drawn shadow does not shimmer with it.

ParticleCloud.metal

compute · 2 kernels
One kernel clears the drawable, one moves and draws 32 motes straight into it. Transparency is the macOS-specific part: non-opaque panel, framebufferOnly = false, clear clearColor, and isOpaque = false on the backing layer. At ramp zero the view pauses after one clearing draw, so an idle HUD costs no GPU.

Where the build stands

Shipped, blocked, deferred

Milestone 1 — the shell plus Direct Dictation — is done and works end to end. The HUD prototyping phase is closed: every option that survived a feel test became a preference instead of being deleted, which is why the inventory above is so wide. The next gate is an insertion-latency benchmark, and it blocks all other features by decision.

What we need from you

Open questions

These are real gaps, not a wish list. Everything above exists; everything below is undecided or unbuilt.

Nine waveform styles, six palettes, three centers

The prototyping phase resolved arguments by shipping every variant as a preference. That is 324 combinations of a single listening state, and the Settings menu now reads like a lab.

Which of these are product, and which were scaffolding? Is there a defensible default set of two or three?

The Siri Orb has to go or be redrawn

It is currently the most distinctive of the three glow centers and it is unlicensed artwork that imitates Apple's own orb. It cannot ship in any form.

Is the orb worth an original replacement, or does the particle cloud carry the identity on its own?

Settings has two sections and a spec calling for six

Dictation, Privacy and Data, System, and an About surface are all written down and empty. The rule is that a section appears only once it has real behaviour behind it.

What is the navigation shape at six sections, and what does the borderless modal header actually contain?

Errors get one line of white text

“Secure field”, missing permissions, and a dead microphone all use the same 15 pt draft slot. Only the dead microphone has a colour of its own.

Do refusals, warnings, and permission prompts need distinct treatments inside a surface this small?

Reduce Motion is a 4 pt bar

It is honest and it is dull. It is also the state a real accessibility user sees for every session, forever.

What does a quiet, motion-free voice visual look like when it is designed rather than reduced?

There is no identity outside the HUD

The menu bar item is a stock SF Symbol microphone that turns red while recording. There is no app icon, no onboarding, no marketing surface.

Where does the brand live for an app whose entire UI is a black shape under a notch?
